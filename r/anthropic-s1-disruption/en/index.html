Summary

  1. The innovation shows up in the numbers, but deceleration has begun. Revenue per dollar of compute rose from $0.40 to $2.08 in 5 quarters, and the company posted its first operating profit in 2Q. But 3Q growth slowed to 45%, and if most one-time revenue fell in 3Q, it drops to 25–33% (about 45% if it was spread evenly). There are also reports that efficiency slipped back to about $1.70.

  2. The risk is fixed costs arriving next year. 80% of revenue is usage-based billing that can shrink at any time. Non-cancelable commitments, by contrast, total about $480–520 billion; spread evenly over the contract terms, 2027 alone comes to about $72.2 billion a year (about 3.3x annualized 2Q compute). Actual spending is back-loaded, so the 2027 figure may be overstated. The three cloud providers on the other side of these commitments are at once shareholders, sales channels (48% of revenue) and competitors.

  3. Adjusted value is about $96–98 per share pre-IPO; $2 trillion is the top of the range. The probability-weighted range is about $1.33–1.99 trillion pre-IPO, centered at about $1.62–1.66 trillion. Read as a post-IPO market cap, $2 trillion is about $113 per share and is justified in only the 2 optimistic scenarios out of 8; investor-indicated prices ($1.4–1.75 trillion) and the roughly $1.2 trillion implied by the end-September fair value of the donation-matching obligation sit below it. Korean retail investors are effectively unable to subscribe to the IPO.

The S-1 states that 3Q revenue was 86x that of 4Q 2024 ($197 million), so the filing itself supports 3Q revenue of about $16.8–17.0 billion [Filing calc.]. But both measures shrank: QoQ growth fell from 144% to 45%, and the increment from about $6.8 billion to about $5.2 billion [Estimate].

3. Wright's Law and the Jevons paradox: efficiency peaked in 2Q and slipped in 3Q

Revenue generated per dollar of compute rose more than 5x, from $0.40 in 1Q 2025 to $2.08 in 2Q 2026, and first crossed the 1.0 line in 1Q 2026 [Filing quarterly table calc.]. But reports say it fell back to about $1.70 in 3Q [Press · FT]. It is unclear whether that is a 3Q-only figure or year-to-date.

  • Wright's Law is not yet proven. Claude Opus 5.5 cut customer cost per task by about 40% versus Opus 5 [Filing]. But one or two generational price cuts are not enough to estimate a learning rate against cumulative output. The denominator of this ratio also includes training compute that arrives in lumps, so it swings from quarter to quarter [Estimate].

  • Evidence of the Jevons paradox: Unit prices fell, yet 2Q revenue rose 1,368% year over year while compute costs rose 300% over the same period [Filing]. The cheaper it gets, the more it is used.

  • Skepticism: This cost line mixes training and research compute with inference. The company disclosed that it 'expects' higher-priced short-term compute usage to rise in the near term and squeeze its operating margin [Filing]. This is a forecast, not a warning. 2.08 may be the peak.

Sources: S-1 original, Fortune S-1 summary, Fortune on compute commitments

4. Unit economics: first operating profit in 2Q, margins slip in 3Q

In 2Q 2026 the company posted its first profit, with operating income of $974 million (8% operating margin), and the adjusted operating margin jumped from -13% in 1Q to 19% in 2Q [Filing quarterly table]. For 3Q, an adjusted operating margin of about 11% has been reported, but the period is unclear. If it is year-to-date, 3Q alone is about 12%; if it is 3Q alone, it is 11%. Either way it slipped from 19% in 2Q [Press · Estimate]. We read this as the short-term compute margin pressure the company disclosed already showing up. The H1 operating margin is -4%, and the $80.7 billion net loss is almost entirely non-cash ($72.4 billion valuation loss + $8.8 billion fair-value change on note purchase agreements).

6. Channels and competition: Amazon and Google are shareholders, suppliers, customers and competitors at once

The share of revenue through marketplaces (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure) rose from 11% in 2023 → 32% in 2024 → 47% in 2025 → 48% in H1 2026. The same channels accounted for 58% of accounts receivable in H1 2026 [Filing]. The company disclosed that the three cloud providers run competing AI models and may favor their own products, and that it has at times not received as much compute as it wanted [Filing].

  • Sunset provisions: The charter defines a 'Class F Sunset Date.' There are several paths to amend the trust provisions. While Class F exists, there are two more paths: two-thirds of the founders + 75% of Class E + a Class A majority, and, until the 3rd anniversary of the IPO, unanimous founders + a Class E majority + a Class A majority. Trigger: if fewer than 3 co-founders remain for 90 consecutive days, a 450-day transition period begins, and the sunset occurs when that period ends or the Class F share is extinguished (redemption or LLC dissolution) [Filing]. Since 5 of the 7 founders must leave to trigger it, and given successor appointments (up to 10 years after the IPO) and the transition period, control could outlast the original founders. Under the founders' LLC agreement, an individual founder can drop out if their Class A holdings fall below 2% of what they held at the IPO or if they are removed, but this is not a cause for extinguishing Class F [Estimate].

  • Hidden dilution mechanism: The company has already contributed 5% on a fully diluted basis (846.84 million shares) to a public benefit fund. The board may contribute up to 2% more each time the share price rises 50% above the previous reference point, and the first reference point is the IPO price [Filing]. Dilution follows as the share price rises.

  • Back-calculation: Since 846.84 million shares are 5%, the fully diluted share count is about 16.9 billion [Estimate]. At $2 trillion, that is about $118 per share [Estimate].

  • Hostile takeover defense: The company warned that this structure lowers the likelihood of unsolicited takeover offers or proxy contests [Filing]. Expectations of a takeover premium should be set at 0.

  • PER: 2030: 35 / 30 / 20x; 2031: 30 / 25 / 18x. Lowered in line with slowing growth, so extending the valuation year does not inflate value.

  • Dilution: Only 1% a year of dilution from stock compensation is applied, because it is already expensed in net income. The IPO is treated as neutral, with proceeds offsetting new shares; additional contributions to the public benefit fund are reflected.

  • Additional bear-case cost: The present value of excess cost on non-cancelable commitments, about -$82 billion to -$97 billion (5 contracts, or including Lambda), is reflected (allocated by contract term).

Why a separate 'aligned' margin: The 2Q cost structure was compute 48%, sales and partnerships 16%, personnel 18% and other 9%, for an operating margin of 8%; for H1 as a whole it was -4% [Filing quarterly table]. A 25% net margin, after income tax (21%), requires the compute ratio to fall to about 19% [Estimate]. Yet under the contract schedules in Section 5, 2027 committed compute cost exceeds 60% of base-scenario revenue. The two assumptions are hard to hold at the same time.

Why convertible notes are not subtracted separately: We did not subtract the $108.6 billion fair value of convertible notes. The fully diluted share count of about 16.9 billion already assumes full conversion of the Amazon convertible notes, so subtracting them would double-count. We also checked in the S-1 whether Google notes or SAFEs sit outside this share count. All notes outstanding as of Jun 30 are Amazon rollover notes, and all SAFEs had converted by 2024, so none remain [Filing]. However, the $77 billion of remaining capacity under the note purchase agreements and the Google concurrent private placement are future new shares outside the fully diluted count.

6 points through an ARK-style innovation lens

  1. Penetration is still low, but the base scenario is already aggressive. Forecast 2028 revenue of $195 billion is about 0.5% of the $40 trillion value of knowledge work the company cites [Estimate]. But a bottom-up estimate starting from $18.6 trillion in wages across 7 knowledge-work occupations (BofA, June 2025) puts total agentic AI spending in 2030 at about $155 billion [Press]. The base scenario's 2030 revenue of $355 billion is more than 2x that entire market. The bull case depends on 'the market being far larger than that estimate.'

  2. Cost curve: deflation is very fast. Epoch AI measured that the cost of reaching the same accuracy has fallen about 47% per quarter (about 13x a year) since 2023. Newly frontier performance falls about 75x a year, slowing to about 4.7x a year after 2 years (published Sep 22, 2026) [Press]. Anthropic also set Opus 5.5's list price 20% below Opus 5 ($4 input and $20 output per 1 million tokens), and cost per task fell by up to about 40% [Filing · Press]. However, a few generational price cuts are not enough to estimate a learning curve against cumulative output (Wright's Law). The data are insufficient (unverified).

  3. Jevons paradox: usage is still winning, but the signals are mixed. While 2Q revenue was 14.7x the prior year, the unit price per task fell about 40%. A rough calculation implies task volume rose about 24x [Estimate]. On the other hand, the 3Q revenue increment shrank from about $6.8 billion to about $5.2 billion [Estimate]. Ramp data show corporate token spending plateaued in August–September, and spending by the top 1% of companies fell 3.1% from July [Press].

  4. Platform convergence. Agents (Code, Cowork) + robotics (controlling lab and manufacturing equipment via the Model Hardware Standard) + life sciences (a market of $2 trillion+ in pharma spending in 2026) overlap on one model [Filing]. Robotics and life-sciences revenue is not disclosed separately, so it is still option value.

  5. Evidence of recursive improvement appears in the filing. In September 2026, Claude wrote more than 90% of codebase contributions. Headcount is about 5x the end-2024 level, while quarterly revenue is 86x [Filing]. More than 80% of 3Q revenue came from model versions released within the prior 6 months, and all top 5 customers posted record 3Q revenue [Filing]. This also means revenue could turn down quickly if releases slip.

  6. The innovation paradox: the price premium is 2x per token and 8x per task. This comparison replaces the earlier 'Fable 5 vs. Grok 4.6' comparison (top tier vs. mid-price tier). GPU rental prices (H100 median) rose 24% over 1 year and 9% over the last 4 weeks [Press]. This points in the same direction as the short-term compute margin pressure the company disclosed.
