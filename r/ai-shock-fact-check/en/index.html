Summary

  • This sell-off is a "funding and permitting crisis," not an "AI demand collapse." B200 GPU rental prices rose 79% in 3 months, and Q4 DRAM contract prices also rose 15–20%. Meanwhile, CoreWeave's 5-year CDS reached 1,038bp, past the line usually seen as distress, and of the 41 data center ballot measures, every one with a result except one went toward restrictions.
  • Three-layer verdict: technology and demand are real (high confidence), business profitability is partly proven (medium), and asset prices and funding structures contain a bubble component (high confidence). OpenAI posted Q2 revenue of $6.7 billion and an operating loss of $12.3 billion.
  • The deciders are Nvidia's earnings on Nov 17 and Big Tech's 2027 capex guidance in late January. Capex growth bends from 78% in 2026 to a consensus 28% in 2027. Scenario probabilities on a 1-year view are range-bound then recovery 45%, deeper credit-driven weakness 30%, quick new highs 25% (author's judgment). We do not predict the bottom.

3. The three-layer verdict

Lump technology, business and asset prices together, and the question "Is AI a bubble?" is framed wrong from the start.

LayerVerdictConfidenceKey evidence
① Technology and demandRealHighNvidia Q2 revenue $96.2 billion (+106%), Q3 guidance $108 billion. GPU rental prices rising
② Business profitabilityPartly provenMediumRevenue surging; operating results in the red, or back in the red after turning a profit
③ Asset prices and funding structureBubble componentHighNeocloud bonds in distressed territory, vendor financing, deteriorating cash flow

① Technology and demand: real. Anthropic's 1H 2026 revenue was $16.3 billion, up sharply from $1.2 billion a year earlier (Built In, based on the S-1), and Q3 revenue was $16.8 billion, about 45% higher than Q2 (FutureSearch, citing CNBC). OpenAI's annualized revenue was about $50 billion at the end of September, with a year-end target of more than $70 billion (Bloomberg, 10/8). The most important evidence is price. In a glut, GPU rental prices should have collapsed first. Instead, they are rising.

② Profitability: partly proven. OpenAI posted Q2 revenue of $6.7 billion and an operating loss of $12.3 billion, and its projected cumulative negative free cash flow for 2026–2030 is $278 billion (FT, 2026.9, citing Sacra). Anthropic swung back to a loss in Q3 after its first operating profit in Q2, and its top 5 customers account for about 25% of revenue in the first 9 months.

③ Asset prices and funding structure: bubble component. CoreWeave's 2031 bond yields 15.78%, and its 5-year CDS spread at one point topped 1,140bp (CNBC, 10/15). The high-yield market was effectively frozen until after the midterms because of the political backlash against data centers (CNBC, 10/21). Nvidia has committed to buy CoreWeave's unsold capacity through April 2032 (InsiderFinance, 10/16).

5. Historical comparisons and disruptive insights

We compared the episode with the dot-com bubble (1999–2002), the telecom bubble (1996–2002), the railway bubble (1840s Britain) and the 2022 tightening.

SimilaritiesDifferences
Vendor financing. The same structure as Lucent's and Nortel's vendor financing in 2000. Nvidia unveiled a financing platform to bring in more than $500 billion of outside capital and even guarantees CoreWeave's unsold capacityReal prices are rising. Fiber bandwidth prices collapsed in 2001, but GPU rental prices and DRAM are rising now. No evidence of excess capacity yet
Cracks in a rate-hike cycle. As in 2000 and 2022, rising rates are fueling sharp drops in AI operators' stocks and bonds and delaying AI infrastructure IPOs (Reuters, October)Revenue is real. Anthropic alone has quarterly revenue of $16.8 billion. Unlike the dot-com era's "growth without revenue"
Extreme concentration. As of June, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix made up about 50% of KOSPI market cap (secondary citation)The brake is political. The technology's own success (power bills, job anxiety) turns into votes that block investment

Five disruptive insights

  1. A gap between the real economy and finance. GPUs and memory are scarce, yet the junk bond market has frozen. A crisis like this breaks first where leverage is highest (neoclouds) and spreads to suppliers. The indicator to watch first is CoreWeave's bonds, more than Nvidia's earnings.
  2. Nvidia is the "buyer of last resort." It funds its customers and even buys the capacity they cannot sell. Part of the demand comes from Nvidia's own balance sheet, and circular risk is concentrated in one place.
  3. AI's success creates the brake. In February, the launch of AI tax planning sent wealth management stocks tumbling on fears of collapsing fees. This time, anxiety over power bills and jobs came back as regulation. OpenAI has already announced it will bear its full power and infrastructure costs.
  1. Korea takes the second-derivative hit twice. Slowing capex growth coincides with slowing memory price growth (80→40→20%). This is the phase where share prices fall even as revenue rises.
  2. Space is inside the AI capex bundle too. S&P Global's 6-company model (early September) included SpaceX's 2027 capex at $197 billion. Space is not a non-AI diversifier.
