The video’s core message (“AI profits are visible, but cash and investment returns are not there yet”) broadly matches Damodaran’s original posts, but some of its numbers are the presenter’s own examples or are wrong.

① Conclusion

Video reliability: medium. The category is not ‘a new industry shift’ but ‘an accounting and cash warning on the AI investment boom.’ The core thesis (hyperscaler CAPEX becomes suppliers’ revenue and profit and inflates market-wide earnings, while cash and investment returns are still unproven) matches Damodaran’s 10/8 blog post. However, the 10 billion example, the 10-year depreciation, “$5 trillion invested,” “$400 billion = the level of US corporate profits” and “invest in AI companies to hedge your human capital” are the presenter’s own examples and interpretations; the last two differ from the original or are wrong.

Is the investment opportunity real? Yes, but it is not a ‘new opportunity.’ Money is already flowing from hyperscalers → GPUs, memory, foundries and power equipment, and the 5 hyperscalers’ Q2’26 CAPEX was about $200B (about $800B annualized, analyst’s sum). The real investment signal in this video is a risk: ‘suppliers’ profits are increasingly funded by their customers’ debt and leases.’

Top research priority: NVIDIA (NVDA); second, SK hynix (000660.KS). NVIDIA has the strongest revenue-to-FCF conversion (CAPEX of $2.7B out of Q2 FY27 OCF of $24.1B), and consensus FY28 EPS already exceeds ‘the 2029 EPS needed for a 10% annual return.’ At the same time, the ‘gray zone’ Damodaran describes, such as equity stakes in customers and $279B in purchase commitments, is largest here, so forensic verification is worth the most.

Power equipment (GEV, LS ELECTRIC, etc.) is the most certain bottleneck, but current prices require 20–55% annual EPS growth, so it is ‘waiting for a price correction.’ This report is not investment advice.

③ Money flow and supply-chain bottlenecks

Spending at the stages below (about $800B a year) is far larger than final demand (AI revenue of up to about $250B), and the gap is filled by hyperscalers’ operating cash, bonds and leases. This gap is the heart of Damodaran’s warning.

We found no primary source that splits, in numbers, how much of this replaces existing markets (budgets shifted from conventional servers and commodity DRAM) and how much is newly created. ⑨ Limits of this analysis and unconfirmed facts

The unconfirmed facts that could change the conclusion most are SK hynix’s cash flow and CAPEX and the terms of its long-term HBM contracts.

  • For the video, we checked only the auto-generated captions and the description and did not see the tables shown on screen. We could not confirm whether the ‘kindergartner’ remark and the ‘$5 trillion·8%’ calculation appear in Damodaran’s YouTube lectures.

  • Korean closing prices are from StockHub pages and may differ from official exchange closes; TSMC’s Taiwan close and the exchange rate are unconfirmed.

  • We could not find OCF·CAPEX for SK hynix and Samsung, Samsung’s FY27 consensus, or confirmed HBM4 contract prices. Cumulative cash in the scenarios is an arbitrary range.

  • NVDA purchase commitments·customer concentration and Broadcom’s customer names come from third-party summaries, not the original 10-Q. SEC EDGAR was blocked in this environment.

  • Anthropic S-1 figures are from reporting on a leaked draft (via Reuters).

  • Sources disagree on whether MSFT’s ‘about $175B’ guidance refers to FY27 or CY26.

  • We could not confirm cash flow, net debt or data center revenue share for the 3 Korean power equipment makers.

  • There is no primary data separating replacement of existing demand from newly created demand.

If we researched only one company in more depth: NVIDIA

It is NVIDIA. It is the profit pool most exposed to this video’s warning (‘supplier profits = builder CAPEX’), and at the same time the only large-cap beneficiary whose consensus already exceeds the growth the current price requires.

Why NVIDIA

  1. Its revenue-to-FCF conversion is the strongest. CAPEX was $2.7B out of quarterly OCF of $24.1B, and its share count fell 1.0% in 1 year.

  2. The price does not demand much. FY28 consensus ($15.91) already exceeds the EPS needed for 10% a year through 2029 ($15.55).

  3. The questions a deep dive can answer are clear. How much the $42.8B in stakes in customers, the $279B in purchase commitments and the jump in debt cloud the quality of revenue can be judged through forensic work on the original 10-Q.

Advantages over the strongest rival candidate, SK hynix

  • Capital intensity: Hynix needs large fab investments to grow and does not disclose CAPEX. NVDA outsources production.

  • Competitive structure: Samsung is closing in on Hynix in HBM, with its share going from 21% in Q1 to 33% in Q2, and commodity prices depend on industry capacity additions.

  • Downside cushion: even in the bear case, NVDA’s net cash and buybacks support per-share value, and its base-case annual return is also higher (+16.2% vs +11.6%).

Where Hynix wins is valuation (about 4x) and more room for a re-rating. If Hynix’s long-term HBM contracts are confirmed with minimum amounts, as Micron’s are, the priority could change.
