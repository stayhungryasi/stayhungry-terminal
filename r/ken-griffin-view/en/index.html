0. Bottom Line

[Inference + synthesis of statements] Keep betting on American strength, but hedge the price it carries: fiscal, inflation and geopolitical risk. Returns come less from market direction than from owning scarce resources (power, compute, liquidity, talent) and harvesting volatility.

How to read the divergence [Interpretation]: The recession warning was a conditional scenario, premised on the strait staying closed for 6–12 months. The Q2 positions read as a bet that the condition would not hold, i.e., on America's relative insulation. But the 13F is an end-of-June snapshot and includes options, so no firm conclusion is possible.

It is a year in which the center of gravity of returns shifted from commodities to equities.

Assets to avoid or hedge [Inference]

  • Semi-liquid private credit sold to individuals. This is the liquidity mismatch he himself warned about.

  • Unhedged long-bond duration. A 5.24% 10-year yield is attractive, but in the bond-vigilante phase he described, the risk of yields rising further remains.

  • Sole reliance on Taiwan supply chains. His estimated GDP −8% scenario is low-probability but amounts to an unbearable loss.

  • Low-margin AI businesses that cannot absorb compute costs. They become the victims of compute inflation.

  • Assets in energy-importing emerging markets. In the May interview he named Pakistan and Bangladesh, which must bear the cost of replacement energy, as the most vulnerable countries (CNBC transcript). That Korea is also an energy importer is taken up again in Section 7.

6. Counterarguments — Why This Reading Could Be Wrong

  1. The 13F is not Griffin's personal view. Citadel is a multi-manager platform that allocates capital to many independent teams, each running its own strategy (Motley Fool). The 13F is therefore the sum of hundreds of PMs' positions. Add options, the long-only disclosure limit and the end-of-June lag. The "divergences" in Sections 2 and 3 may be a simple aggregation effect rather than a contradiction.

  2. The energy expansion may be defense, not offense. The commodities unit earned $8 billion in 2022 and $4 billion a year in 2023–2024. But it lagged in 2025, and firms such as Jane Street and QRT are moving into physical gas trading (Bloomberg/Energy Connects). A star trader is leaving, too. If so, the Paloma and FlexPower deals are moves to protect a shrinking profit pool and may not extend to case A (vertical integration all the way to compute).

  3. Many of the statements are policy advocacy. Remarks on nuclear power, data center siting, fiscal discipline and Fed independence may be political and policy messages more than portfolio signals. The Miami investments also have a strong civic character, through Griffin Catalyst. And the April recession warning was conditional on the strait staying closed for 6–12 months; the S&P has since set new record highs. Using his macro remarks as trading signals can lead you astray.
