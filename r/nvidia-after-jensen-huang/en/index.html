Jensen Huang, the founder who led Nvidia for 33 years, stepped down on Nov 12. Two days later the stock closed at $162.85, 31.9% below its record close. Analyst earnings estimates are intact and the whole decline came from the multiple: the stock trades at 10.3 times expected FY28 earnings. The idea that rivals would benefit broke when they fell harder on Nov 13. We map the risks, scenarios and dates to watch.

BRIEF

  • On Nov 12 Nvidia's board said Jensen Huang, the founder who led the company for 33 years, had resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. The stock closed at $162.85 on Nov 13, 31.9% below the record close of $239.24 on Oct 6.
  • The analyst consensus for FY28 EPS held at about $15.85. The decline came from the multiple, not from earnings; the stock trades at 10.3 times expected FY28 earnings. Market-implied EPS is $12.53 (author's calculation), below the lowest analyst estimate of $12.85.

After the resignation, Reuters reported an alleged relationship with another employee.

Market-implied EPS is the $162.85 close divided by the 13x multiple Stifel applied after the exit. It is 20.9% below consensus and below the lowest analyst estimate. The five-year average 12-month forward P/E was 36x. Most estimates predate the exit, however, and may be cut after the Nov 17 earnings report.

4. What changed: rivals did not benefit

Until early trading on Nov 12, AMD and Broadcom were rising. On Nov 13 AMD fell 8.75% and Broadcom 4.76%, both more than Nvidia. One reading is that the market began to price a risk to the whole AI investment chain rather than a shift in share (author's judgment). Nvidia has supported customers' data-center buildouts with guarantees and commitments; as of its July 2026 quarterly filing, customer-support commitments total $164.5 billion.

Why this could be wrong

  • Whether the company itself bears responsibility cannot be judged from public information.
  • If estimates are cut after earnings, the denominator behind 'cheap' changes.
  • Strong results plus a quick CEO appointment could erase the discount sooner than assumed.

7. A disruption lens: Nvidia is already the incumbent

Nvidia's share of AI accelerator revenue is estimated to slip from about 87% in 2024 to about 75% in 2026 (Silicon Analysts). For 2026, shipments of custom ASICs are forecast to grow 44.6% against 16.1% for merchant GPUs (TrendForce). The erosion started before this episode. That a leadership gap speeds it up is a hypothesis, and the sharp fall in rivals on Nov 13 does not fit it. Share estimates range from 70% to 87% depending on the tracker.
