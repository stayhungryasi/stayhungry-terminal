The question the market is asking has shifted from "Is AI demand real?" to "Who borrows the money to fund that demand, and at what price?" As a result, the ranking of superintelligence bets has also changed.

Data as of October 23, 2026 (US close) · Previous analysis September 11, 2026 (two days after the ARK swap)

Labeling rules: "filing" means company filings and IR; "reported" means press reports; "consensus" means aggregated analyst estimates; "estimate" means the author's calculation; "unverified" means the original source could not be confirmed. One error in the first analysis of September 11 is recorded as-is in Section 2.

Summary

Conclusion: In the superintelligence bet, there are still alternatives with a better expected return relative to risk than Meta. But the ranking has changed. Alphabet, which we ranked No. 1 in September, drops to No. 4, while Nvidia and SK hynix move up. GE Vernova, from the power layer, falls out of the top 5.

The ranking changed not because of company results but because of the market's question. In September the market asked "Is AI demand real?", and cloud +82% and a $514 billion backlog were the answer. In October the market asks "Who borrows the money to fund that demand, and at what price?" The Fed raised rates by 25bp on September 16, taking the policy rate to 3.75–4.00%, and the 10-year yield is in the 5.1–5.3% range (reported, 10/20). On October 21, Meta announced quarterly FCF of -$2.1 billion along with the creation of non-voting Class C shares "for capital raising" (filing). The next day GE Vernova fell -11%, Vertiv -7% and Eaton -6% (reported, 10/22). That day marks the regime shift. AI capex is now read not as evidence of demand but as evidence of borrowing.

Three kinds of companies have the edge in this regime: those growing on their own cash (Nvidia, TSMC), those whose multiples have been pressed to extremes by cycle fears (SK hynix), and those exposed to frontier labs through equity stakes rather than their balance sheet (Amazon). At a disadvantage are those increasing capex while FCF is negative (Alphabet, Meta, Oracle) and highly valued power equipment names (GE Vernova at 36.5x, Vertiv at about 41x).

1. Re-checking the starting point — Is Cathie Wood ahead after 6 weeks?

The swap itself. On September 9, ARK bought 43,091 META shares (about $27.9 million) and sold 84,392 GOOGL shares (about $27.8 million) (ARK daily disclosure). ARK kept buying META afterward — about 11,000 shares on 9/14, about 51,500 shares on 9/21, and about 32,300 shares on 10/13–14 (about $24.4 million) (reported). No further GOOGL sales were confirmed. We found no statement in which Cathie Wood directly explained the logic of this swap (unverified). The "superintelligence bet" interpretation used by the press is the reporters' inference.

Performance. META rose +17% from $653.69 on September 9 to about $762 on October 13, then gave back -7% after earnings on October 21, closing at $642.62 (-1.7%) on October 23. GOOGL is at $320.21 (-3.2%) after its October 9 high of $351.66. The ARK swap is ahead by 1.5%p. The gap is not large, but a trade the author saw on September 11 as having a "65% chance of being wrong" has not yet been wrong.

Meta Q3 (10/21, filing). Revenue $64.6 billion (+26%, above consensus of $63.3 billion). GAAP EPS was $3.83, but excluding a $9.89 billion provision tied to the August settlement with 52 states over youth mental health, it was $7.40. Operating income $22.88 billion, expenses +68%. Capex $34.91 billion (+80%); full-year guidance $135–145 billion (low end raised by $5 billion). FCF -$2.12 billion — the first negative quarter since listing. Q4 revenue guidance $73–76 billion (consensus about $73.6 billion). Headcount 66,775 (-15%). Meta will create non-voting Class C shares via a 1:1 stock dividend around January 19, 2027, and explicitly stated the purpose as "capital raising." On the same day, a New Mexico court ordered a $25 billion fine related to Cambridge Analytica, and Meta plans to appeal (reported) — this is not reflected in the Q3 numbers. The Muse agent has 10 million accounts in the US and Canada, with subscriptions at $20/$100 per month, and Zuckerberg mentioned "cutting serving costs by 10x or more within a year" (reported). There is no revenue contribution figure yet.

Alphabet reports Q3 after the close on October 28. Consensus: revenue about $127.9 billion (+25%), EPS about $3.02, cloud +89% (BofA +93%); whether the 2026 capex guidance of $195–205 billion is maintained; 2027 capex (BMO $325 billion, Wolfe $330 billion); 2027 FCF consensus about -$32 billion (reported). A possible valuation loss on its SpaceX stake (about 551 million shares) is also a variable (reported, BofA 10/20).

Re-estimating the probability that Cathie Wood is right — 35% → 45% (author's judgment). Why we raised it: (1) On forward P/E, META (19.5x) is cheaper than GOOGL (23.9x) — "Meta is more expensive," the core argument of the first analysis, was wrong (Section 2). (2) With Meta ads +26% and Q4 guidance in line with consensus, the ad engine is intact. (3) GOOGL's 2027 FCF consensus of -$32 billion puts it at a greater disadvantage in a funding regime. Why we did not raise it further: Meta's first negative FCF, the new Class C shares and the $25 billion fine are downside specific to Meta, and Alphabet has not yet reported Q3. We expect Alphabet's October 28 results to move this probability again by 10%p or more.

2. Corrections to the first analysis

Basis for the P/E comparison. The September 11 analysis took "GOOG P/E 17 vs META 23" straight from press reports and used it to argue that "Meta is more expensive." That 17x was on a trailing GAAP basis, and Alphabet's Q2 EPS of $9.11 included equity valuation gains on Anthropic, SpaceX and others (about $28.7 billion in Q1 alone, reported 4/30). Excluding valuation gains and using a forward basis, GOOGL is at 23.9x and META at 19.5x (StockAnalysis 10/23) — a reversal. The logic that "a P/E of 17 limits the downside" rested on weak grounds. The rest of the Alphabet thesis (cloud growth, external TPU sales, Waymo, full stack) stands, but we withdraw the "it's cheap" argument. This correction explains half of the ranking change.

Assessment. In the compute layer, the demand debate is over. Nvidia's FY28 revenue growth guidance of about +70% assumes supply constraints, and capex by its top 5 customers is expected to grow from about $800 billion in 2026 to $1.3 trillion in 2027 (Nvidia call 8/26). TSMC plans to more than double N2 capacity in 2027 and hinted it may raise its AI accelerator growth guidance from "the mid-to-high 50% range" (10/15). Yet every name fell 5–18% in a month. The cause is not results but rates (10-year at 5.1% or higher) and neocloud credit (CoreWeave 2032 bonds yielding about 15%; stock -12% on 10/16).

Memory has a separate debate. Micron's GM of 86.8%, Samsung's company-wide operating margin of 55% and SK hynix's consensus operating margin of 78% are numbers never seen in manufacturing, and the rationale for KOSPI's -5.46% on October 13 (SK hynix -10.1%) was "peak-out." However, Q4 DRAM contract prices are still rising at +20% (TrendForce 10/20, slowing from +80% in Q2 and +40% in Q3), and 2027 HBM average selling prices are expected at +121% (TrendForce). Samsung is negotiating HBM4 with Nvidia and Broadcom at more than 2x the price of HBM3E (reported, 10/6). A forward P/E of 3.75x means the market has already priced in 2027 earnings being cut in half. SK hynix has signed long-term supply agreements, typically 5 years, with about 10 customers (7/29). Even if earnings are halved, the P/E is 7.5x.

Assessment. In September we put GEV at No. 3 as "the axis that sells even if ASI doesn't arrive." The backlog logic still holds — gas turbine prices are $600 per kW by end-2027 (+195% vs. 2019, WoodMac), and lead times for large transformers are 128–144 weeks. But two things have changed. First, the -11% on October 22 showed that GEV trades like a derivative of news about Meta's capex funding — the fact that "80% of customers are non-AI" did not protect the stock. Second, WoodMac sees gas turbine demand peaking in 2026 (4/1). Combined with the consensus for 2027 FCF of -35% as customer deposits normalize, a forward 36.5x and EV/EBITDA of 60x are hard to justify. We drop GEV from the top 5. Names exposed to the same backlog curve at less than half the multiple are Constellation (about 19x, 3.59GW Google deal, about -28% YTD) and HD Hyundai Electric (2027E about 14x).

5. Assessment of META and GOOGL

META — neutral. Ad revenue +26%, Q4 guidance in line with consensus, and at 19.5x forward it is cheaper than Alphabet — this is where the September analysis was wrong. Still, there are three reasons we do not raise our view: the first negative FCF (-$2.1 billion) and the raised capex floor, the new Class C shares with "capital raising" stated explicitly (a dilution signal), and the $25 billion New Mexico fine not reflected in Q3. Its structural weakness as a superintelligence bet also remains — with no channel to sell compute or models externally, ASI's profits come back only through a single path of ads and agents. Muse's 10 million accounts are a starting point, not evidence.

GOOGL — thesis valid, conviction high → medium. October 28 results are the deciding point. Grounds for keeping it: cloud consensus +89%, external TPU sales (Mizuho models $100 billion in TPU revenue in 2028), Waymo in 15 cities with a target of 20,000 vehicles by 2027, an Anthropic stake of about 14% (about 7% of market cap at a $2 trillion IPO), and avoiding an AdX breakup in the ad tech lawsuit (9/16). Grounds for lowering it: the "it's cheap" argument withdrawn (23.9x forward), 2027 FCF consensus of -$32 billion, and -9% from the October 9 high. What to watch on October 28: cloud at 85% or more, the scale of TPU revenue recognized, 2027 capex of $330 billion or less, and any mention of when FCF turns. If three or more of the four appear, the thesis is restored; if two or fewer, we lower conviction further.

6. What it takes for the opposite of ARK (GOOGL ahead) to be right

  1. Alphabet on October 28 — cloud at 85% or more + disclosure of external TPU sales figures + 2027 capex of $330 billion or less. With this combination, the market re-reads Alphabet's capex as "backlog fulfillment" rather than "borrowing," and 23.9x forward is justified. If cloud falls below 80%, the opposite side loses.
  2. Anthropic lists in mid-November at $1.5 trillion or more. Alphabet's roughly 14% stake would be valued at $210 billion or more. Meta has no stake in a frontier lab. The same event also works for Amazon (No. 3) and Broadcom.
  3. Meta actually issues Class C shares or books additional legal provisions. If the $25 billion fine is provisioned or real capital is raised through Class C, the "it's cheap" argument of 19.5x forward is offset by dilution.

Conversely, the conditions under which Cathie Wood is right are an Alphabet cloud slowdown + 2027 capex above $330 billion + disclosure of a Muse revenue contribution figure. If all three appear together, the author lowers the GOOGL view to neutral.

7. Briefing summary

Six weeks after ARK's September 9 trade of buying Meta and selling Alphabet, Meta is ahead by 1.5%p. Both stocks have fallen: Meta is about -16% from its October 13 high and Alphabet about -9% from its October 9 high. The author's September view of a "65% chance Cathie Wood is wrong" has not yet been verified, and we lower it to 55%. There are two reasons. First, the "Alphabet P/E 17 vs. Meta 23" used in the September analysis was on a trailing basis that included Alphabet's equity valuation gains; on a forward basis it flips to Meta 19.5x and Alphabet 23.9x. Second, the market's question has changed. With the Fed raising rates and the 10-year above 5%, when Meta announced its first negative quarterly FCF and new shares for capital raising on October 21, GE Vernova, Vertiv and Eaton fell 6–11% the next day. AI capex is now read not as evidence of demand but as evidence of borrowing.

In this regime, the five superintelligence bets the author sees as better than Meta are Nvidia, SK hynix, Amazon, Alphabet and TSMC. Nvidia is at 23.6x forward with FY28 earnings consensus of +71%, for a PEG of 0.33, and it grows on its own cash rather than other people's money. SK hynix is -57% from its June high at 3.75x forward; even if 2027 earnings halve, it is 7.5x — Q4 DRAM contract prices are still +20%, and HBM4 is being negotiated at more than 2x the price of HBM3E. Amazon is cheaper than Alphabet at 19x forward while holding both an Anthropic stake and a $50 billion commitment to OpenAI — if Anthropic lists at $2 trillion in mid-November, more than 10% of Amazon's market cap could be re-rated at once. Alphabet still packs cloud, TPU, Waymo and an Anthropic stake into one stock, but it has lost the "it's cheap" argument and its 2027 FCF consensus is -$32 billion, so it drops to No. 4. GE Vernova is dropped despite a $176 billion backlog because of 36.5x forward and a 2027 FCF consensus of -35%; Constellation (about 19x) and HD Hyundai Electric (about 14x), with the same power backlog at less than half the multiple, go on the watch list. Oracle stays excluded because of its S&P BBB- rating.

The next deciding point is October 28. If Alphabet's results deliver three of cloud at 85% or more, a TPU revenue figure and 2027 capex of $330 billion or less, the Alphabet thesis is restored; if cloud is below 80%, Cathie Wood was right.
