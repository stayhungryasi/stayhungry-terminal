Conclusion: gradual recovery over 1 year (55% confidence), upside bias over 3 years (60% confidence)

Ethereum is cementing its place as the settlement layer of institutional finance (stablecoins and tokenization), but the link that turns that success into ETH price (fees and burn) has weakened. The 1-year base case is around $3,000 (probability-weighted expected value +14%), and the 3-year base case is around $4,000 (expected value +71%). The biggest variable for the 1-year outlook, however, is the demand gap that will open if BMNR stops buying within the next 4–6 weeks.

5. Technology roadmap and competition

Technically, Ethereum has entered a phase of greatly expanding its capacity. But whether ETH captures the value of that added capacity is a separate question.

Technology roadmap

  • Glamsterdam upgrade: live on the Sepolia testnet on Oct 6, with mainnet targeted for Q4 2026. It aims to sharply raise mainnet throughput through a redesign of block production (ePBS) and efficiency gains. Possible attacks by block producers were flagged on the testnet, and the schedule could slip, as it has in the past.

  • Fusaka (completed Dec 2025): an attempt to restore the burn by introducing a price floor for layer-2 data fees.

  • The long-term goal is throughput of about 10,000 transactions per second by 2030. Many analyses note, however, that Glamsterdam by itself will not bring an overall cut in fees.

Paths to collapse (combined probability about 3% over 1 year, about 5% over 3 years)

  1. Major protocol failure: a consensus bug or large-scale staking slashing. The risk grows as upgrades become more frequent.

  2. Regulatory shock: policy reverses so that ETH is classified as a security or staking is banned.

  3. Cascade selling: if treasury companies start selling their ETH while trading at a discount, a vicious circle could put 6.6% of supply on the market. BMNR currently has no debt, so pressure for forced selling is low.

  4. Crypto-wide crisis: a 2022-style event, such as the collapse of a major exchange or stablecoin.

Conclusion on BMNP: its ability to pay survives even the collapse scenario, but its price could fall into the $60s.

11. 3 ways this outlook could be wrong, and what to monitor

Ways it could be wrong

  1. If the failure of value capture becomes entrenched: usage grows, but layer 2s and Solana take the fees and MEV. If the market re-rates ETH as "infrastructure that earns no rent," the monetary premium shrinks and fair value is pulled toward method ① (fee-based).

  2. Demand cliff: BMNR's buying ends at the same time as ETF outflows, and staking ETFs fail to fill the gap.

  3. Macro or regulatory shock: a stock market plunge, a spike in rates or a reversal in regulation would push ETH down regardless of fundamentals.
