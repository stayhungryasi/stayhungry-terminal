Summary

Conclusion: AI's bottleneck has shifted from 'GPUs alone' to a simultaneous shortage of 'chips, memory and usable MW (power delivery)'. The places most certain to get paid are advanced foundry/packaging, HBM, and oligopoly power equipment with 3–5 year lead times (large gas turbines, UHV transformers, power distribution).

  • The skeleton of Musk's remarks is right; some of the numbers are exaggerated. Google's and Anthropic's leasing of SpaceX compute (F) is confirmed by SEC filings. By contrast, 'power supply outside China growing 10–20% a year' (D) and 'humanoids making the world economy 10x' (B) are not supported by independent data.

  • The most important remark is the 15GW. Musk himself wrote on X on Aug 29 that the core issue is not generation but 'turning on' the chips: transformers, wiring, liquid cooling, chillers and networking. In other words, the bottleneck is the power delivery chain more than power plants.

  • Scarcity rent already shows up in prices. The $920 million a month Google pays SpaceX works out to about $11.5 per GPU-hour on 110,000 GPUs, several times the 2025 H100 rental rate. A GPU connected to electricity has become the most expensive asset.

  • Memory is a co-bottleneck. SK hynix posted a 76% operating margin in Q2 2026, and its 2026 HBM volume is sold out. That said, peak margins are a warning sign in the cycle.

  • AI revenue has started to catch up with CAPEX. Anthropic's annualized revenue was $65 billion at end-July (about $9 billion at end-2025), and OpenAI's about $40 billion. This strengthens the case for the Big 4's 2026 CAPEX in the $700 billion range.

  • What the market has priced in less: In a power-constrained era, chip growth eventually converges to the 'growth rate of operating MW'. Long-term value therefore partly shifts from chips to the power delivery chain. At the same time, NVIDIA trades at about 24x forward P/E despite FY28 revenue guidance of +70%, so the market is discounting how long its growth lasts.

One-sentence conclusion: The places most likely to make the most money in the AI revolution are those with 'the ability to actually turn chips on': advanced foundry, HBM, and oligopoly power delivery equipment (gas turbines, transformers, distribution). The reason: as AI usage grows, chip demand grows, but the speed at which that becomes revenue is set by usable MW, and the equipment that creates that MW is an oligopoly market with 3–5 year lead times, so pricing power sits with the suppliers.

4. The CAPEX Chain Created by $1 of AI Revenue

Conclusion: In steady state, producing $1 of annual AI end revenue requires about $1.6–2.4 of cumulative equipment (installed base), of which about $0.8–1.1 is semiconductors. In power terms, that is about 0.3–0.5kWh per $1. The actual 2026 ratio is 2–3x higher because of front-loaded investment.

Model assumptions (per 1GW of IT load, 2026 prices):

  • Total CAPEX for a 1GW AI data center about $45–55 billion: chips and systems $33–38 billion, facility power and cooling $10–13 billion, land and buildings $2–3 billion; with self-generation, gas turbines at about $2,000–2,500 per kW ($2–2.5 billion per 1GW).

  • Annual end AI revenue per 1GW (apps, API and cloud combined) about $20–30 billion. Compute rents alone, with today's scarcity rent attached, run at $40–50 billion a year per GW (backed out of the Anthropic-SpaceX contract). On normalization we assume less than half that.

Final Summary (Final Page)

① The 5 most important structural changes

  1. The unit of the bottleneck has changed from 'chips' to 'usable MW' (Google→SpaceX about $11.5 per GPU-hour).

  2. Memory has become a bottleneck on par with GPUs (SK hynix operating margin 76%).

  3. On-site generation that bypasses the grid has become a standard option (xAI Southaven, Doosan turbines).

  4. Compute has become a leasable commodity (SpaceX contracts about $26 billion annualized).

  5. AI revenue has started to catch up with CAPEX (Anthropic $65 billion annualized, 7x vs end-2025).

② The 5 strongest AI Toll Booths: advanced foundry + packaging, GPU platforms, HBM, the large gas turbine oligopoly, UHV transformers.

③ TOP 10 for the next 3 years: NVIDIA, TSMC, Broadcom, SK hynix, GE Vernova, Lumentum, Siemens Energy, Vertiv, HD Hyundai Electric, Hitachi.

④ TOP 10 compounders for 5–10 years: TSMC, NVIDIA, Broadcom, GE Vernova, Hitachi, Schneider Electric, Eaton, Siemens Energy, Constellation, Quanta Services.

⑤ Hidden Winner TOP 10: Solaris Energy Infrastructure, Powell Industries, Sanil Electric, BE Semiconductor, Astera Labs, Fujikura, BHI, ISU Petasys, Delta Electronics, Taihan Cable & Solution.

⑥ The 5 riskiest AI consensus trades

  1. Chasing memory at peak margins (Samsung, Micron, CXMT capacity additions in 2027–2028).

  2. GPU leasing and neoclouds that put 5-year depreciation against 3-year contracts, and high-multiple SpaceX.

  3. Pure SMR themes with no revenue before 2030.

  4. Data center developers and REITs carrying a 'power-secured site' premium (oversupply if efficiency improves).

  5. Humanoid theme stocks pricing in 2030s value in a 50,000-unit-a-year market.

⑦ 5 things the market does not yet know

  1. Power constraints are the ceiling on chip growth. After 2028, chip shipment growth converges to growth in operating MW.

  2. Even so, NVIDIA trades at about 24x forward P/E against FY28 +70% guidance; the market has over-priced a growth cliff.

  3. Gas turbines are a true toll, with 20–30 years of service revenue attached after the sale.

  4. Grid connection queues (4+ years) structurally boost demand for on-site generation and distribution.

  5. The biggest beneficiaries of humanoids may be memory and power, not actuators (1 billion units would use about 10% of world power).

  1. Big 4 quarterly CAPEX guidance and 2027 commentary

  2. NVIDIA comments on supply constraints, Vera Rubin mix
