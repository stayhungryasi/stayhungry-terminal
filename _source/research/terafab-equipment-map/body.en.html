Summary

Conclusion: Terafab is a real project, but its investment horizon is 10 years and Phase 1 is small, so near-term equipment demand is smaller than the market expects. On irreplaceability and certainty, ASML ranks first. The benefit the market is overlooking is mask inspection and metrology equipment, driven by the in-house mask shop and a new organization's yield learning.

  • Near-term equipment demand is smaller than it looks. The $119.4 billion total is spread over 4 phases through 2036, and Phase 1 is $16.8 billion. Phase 1 equipment demand is estimated at about 3–4% of annual global WFE. For large global toolmakers, Terafab is closer to an "option" than a driver of earnings.

  • ASML ranks first on irreplaceability and certainty. The hidden winners are Lasertec and KLA (in-house mask making + a new organization's yield learning). In Korea, only one company has a confirmed purchase order (PO): HPSP. The rest are at the NDA or discussion stage.

  • Korean beneficiary candidates have corrected sharply but are still expensive. After the 10/15 plunge they stand -45 to -53% from their highs, yet their P/E is still 49–85x. Terafab alone can hardly explain current valuations.

4. Hidden beneficiaries the market is missing

  1. The in-house mask shop chain [Estimate] Making masks in the same building is a feature no other fab has. It requires mask inspection (Lasertec, KLA, ZEISS) and multi-beam mask writers (IMS, majority-owned by Intel). In other words, Intel earns equipment revenue through IMS on top of its process license. In Korea, blank masks (S&S Tech) and pellicles (FST) could be linked, but neither company has any reported contact; this is a pure hypothesis.

  2. A new organization's yield-learning costs [Estimate] A team with no fab experience buys more metrology and inspection tools per wafer and uses them longer. Companies with a large share of service revenue, such as KLA and Nova, benefit.

  3. China excluded + Japan expensive = Korea as an alternative supplier [Confirmed + Estimate] [Confirmed] Tesla and SpaceX do not deal with Chinese vendors. The industry view is that once China is excluded few suppliers remain, and Japan is expensive. [Estimate] This setup helps Korean materials, parts and equipment suppliers get in but hurts their margins (see 5-2).

  4. Texas water and environmental infrastructure [Estimate] A Tesla facilities manager asked directly about wastewater reuse equipment (G&G Intech). Unisem has signed an NDA for scrubbers. Companies with no reported contact should not be lumped in as Terafab beneficiaries just because they are in the same category.

5. Risks

5-1. Companies whose case collapses if the partner changes

  • [Confirmed] According to THE ELEC, ENF Technology, Asflow, ISC and HPSP came in through Intel's supplier route. Dongjin Semichem came in through purchasing staff formerly at GlobalFoundries.

  • [Estimate] A switch to a TSMC process would change recipes and material specs and require requalification. The more a company was mentioned because of Intel's supply chain, the bigger its risk.

  • [Confirmed] SpaceX's IPO filing states that neither Tesla nor Intel has any obligation to stay in Terafab.

5-2. Pricing, timeline and idle equipment

  • Pricing: [Confirmed] According to a supplier official, Terafab is asking for "prices lower than China's."

  • Idle equipment: [Estimate] SemiAnalysis expects the main building to be completed in late 2027, with tool installation delayed until after that. If EUV tools arrive first, they may sit unused for a while.

  • Funding: [Confirmed] Tesla's free cash flow was negative in Q2, and how the two companies will split costs has not been disclosed. We judge funding to be a risk the market underestimates.

  • Macro: [Confirmed] On 10/15 the KOSPI plunged by more than 7%. The causes were fears of rate hikes after US September CPI came in at 3.7% (above expectations), and worries about AI investment debt.

One-sentence conclusion: Terafab is real, but it is a 10-year project and Phase 1 is small, so the benefits confirmed today are limited to HPSP, which holds a PO, and the unavoidable monopolies (ASML, Lasertec). For large global toolmakers it is an option on top of the AI investment cycle; for Korean companies still in talks, the case is complete only once order filings appear.
