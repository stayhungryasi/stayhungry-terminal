Summary

  1. The IEA expects global LNG supply to fall 3% (about 20 bcm) in 2026, assuming the Hormuz disruption lasts through year-end. That would be the steepest annual drop on record. On Nov 12 Asia's JKM was $29.67 and Europe's TTF $28.33, while US Henry Hub was $3.25 (per MMBtu).
  2. The structural loss is 17% of Qatar's export capacity (12.8 million tonnes a year) and takes about 3 years to repair. QatarEnergy expects the other 12 trains back in about 2 months once the strait is safe. In October 31 LNG tankers left the Gulf, the most since the war began.
  3. US LNG exporters' 2026 earnings are largely locked in. Venture Global raised guidance to $10.0–10.3 billion and Cheniere to $8.75–9.25 billion. In the author's judgment, equipment makers whose backlog stays even if gas prices fall (Baker Hughes IET backlog: $39.4 billion) are the more structural winners.

1. Is it a global issue

Yes, with the United States as the exception. Before the war about 20% of global LNG trade passed through the Strait of Hormuz, which has been effectively closed since the war began in late February. Europe entered winter with low storage and competes with Asia for spot cargoes.

A partial recovery has begun. In October 31 tankers carried LNG out of the Gulf, up from 23 in September and the most since the war began. The UAE is back to prewar levels and Qatar is just over 30% (S&P Global count). Force majeure is extended to late November or early December. On Nov 10 Poland's Orlen became the first buyer to cancel a long-term contract with QatarEnergy.

In the author's judgment the most structural layer is bottleneck equipment. Repairs, new plants and AI power all compete for the same turbine and compressor production slots, so the backlog stays even if gas prices fall. The spread layer is the most direct, but 2026 is already locked in and 2027 can go either way depending on Hormuz. Shipyards have strong earnings from ships booked at higher prices, while prices and share in new orders are weakening.

5. Who is hurt

  • Gas-intensive manufacturers in Korea: under Korea Gas Corporation's Nov 1 tariff the industrial city-gas wholesale price is KRW 26.5028 per MJ, above the frozen residential price of KRW 20.8495. Maeil Business Newspaper (Nov 10) reported that the industrial price rose 65.9% in a year.
  • Independent power producers: the same report says margins shrink because wholesale power prices do not keep up with fuel costs. Company-level figures could not be checked in this review (unverified).
  • Demand destruction: the IEA expects global gas demand to fall about 1.5% (65 bcm) in 2026. Gains for nuclear, coal and electrification are a directional inference; individual companies were not checked.

8. Checkpoints

  • Qatar transits back to 50%: now about 30%. Above that, downward pressure on spot prices grows.
  • Force majeure lifted: whether it is extended again sets the direction of winter prices.
  • Venture Global's 2027 guidance: due with the next quarterly results. It shows whether the spread gain carries into next year.
