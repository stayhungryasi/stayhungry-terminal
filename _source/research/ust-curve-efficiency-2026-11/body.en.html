Summary

  1. On the Nov 20 close, reward per unit of risk (excess return over the 3-month bill ÷ modified duration) is highest for the 2-year at 0.23. That is 1.8x the 20-year's 0.12 and 3.6x the 30-year's 0.06. It is a calculation that assumes an unchanged curve, not a forecast.
  2. The 30-year trails the 20-year on all three measures. Yield is 5.31% vs 5.34%, modified duration 14.92 vs 12.20, and rolldown −0.04pp vs +0.50pp. In absolute terms the 20-year has the highest expected return on the curve at 5.84%.
  3. The edge is conditional. The 2-year's breakeven cushion fell from 72bp to 44bp in one week, and it loses to cash if yields rise more than 44bp. With the 1-year as the cash benchmark, the edge over the 20-year shrinks to 1.2x.

2. Efficiency by maturity: who pays more for the same risk

The return from holding a bond for one year is the sum of yield (carry) and rolldown. Rolldown is the price gain as the bond's maturity shortens by a year and it moves into a lower-yield part of the curve. Subtract the 3-month bill yield of 4.32% to get excess return; divide that by modified duration to get reward per unit of risk.

The cushion is how far yields can rise over one year before the return equals the 3-month bill. The range is the high minus the low from Oct 20 to Nov 20. Only the 2-year has a cushion larger than its range, and narrowly: 44 vs 42.

3. Curve shape: steep for 2 years, flat for 28

From the 3-month bill to the 2-year, yields rise 31bp; from the 2-year to the 30-year they rise 68bp. The slope per year is 17.7bp in the front segment and 2.4bp in the back, a 7.3x difference. Of the total 99bp rise, 31% sits in the first 2 years.

The peak is the 20-year at 5.34%; the 30-year is 3bp lower at 5.31%. Note that the chart on card 2 starts its vertical axis at 4.2%, so the slope looks steeper than it is.

4. Dominance: which maturity loses to another on everything

The 30-year has a lower yield than the 20-year (5.31% vs 5.34%), a longer modified duration (14.92 vs 12.20) and negative rolldown (−0.04pp vs +0.50pp). One-year expected return is 5.27% vs 5.84%. It is behind on all three.

The 3-year has a lower expected return than the 2-year (4.69% vs 4.75%) with 1.5x the duration. The 5-year's expected return of 4.82% is 0.07pp above the 2-year's, with 2.3x the duration.

6. Counter-case: what breaks this conclusion

  1. The cushion is shrinking fast. The 2-year's cushion was 72bp on Nov 16, 58bp on the 19th and 44bp on the 20th. As the 2-year yield fell from 4.83% to 4.63%, the remaining reward shrank.
  2. The front-end slope may be hike expectations rather than term compensation (author's view). The Fed's target range is 3.75–4.00% (Oct 28 statement), and as of Nov 17 the OIS market was pricing a 25bp hike at the Dec 9 meeting (rateprobability.com). If hikes arrive as priced, the rolldown gain does not materialize. Pricing after Nov 20 is unverified.
  3. The edge depends on the cash benchmark. Against the 1-year at 4.51% instead of the 3-month bill, the 2-year's reward per risk is only 1.2x the 20-year's.
  4. The 20-year's high yield may be compensation for low liquidity (author's view). If so, its rolldown of 0.50pp may not be realized.

7. Verification log

  1. A draft as of Nov 19 presented the reward per risk at 2 years and under as '4.2x the 30-year'. That compared against the worst maturity, so it was changed to the 20-year. As of Nov 20 it is 1.8x the 20-year and 3.6x the 30-year.
  2. The draft's statement that '2 years and under holds up even if yields rise 100bp' was wrong. The 2-year loses to cash once its cushion is exceeded; at +100bp it returns 3.8%, below the 3-month bill's 4.32%. Only the 1-year, which matures at 4.51%, holds up.
  3. The Treasury table for the 24 trading days from Oct 16 to Nov 19 × 10 maturities was fetched twice and compared, with no mismatches. The calculations were re-derived with a differently written pricing formula.

8. Checkpoints

  • FOMC decision on Dec 9. If the move differs from what is priced, the front end moves first.
  • The gap between the 3-month bill and the 2-year (31bp on Nov 20). The narrower it gets, the smaller the reward at 2 years and under.
  • The 20-year / 30-year inversion (currently 3bp). If it ends, the dominance changes.

9. Method and limits

Assumptions: par bonds, semiannual coupons, linear interpolation between maturities, a 12-month holding period. Coupon reinvestment income, taxes, trading costs and FX are not included. Expected return is arithmetic on an unchanged curve, not an outlook.

Term premium estimates and Treasury auction metrics could not be confirmed (unverified). FX hedging cost could not be verified and is not covered here.
