Summary

  1. Money is moving from "security powered by AI" to "security for AI." Gartner expects global security spending of $244.2B (+13.3%) in 2026. Yet spending to protect AI itself is only $2.8B, just 1/17 of AI-powered security ($49B). This gap is the one likely to close fastest over the next 3–5 years.

  2. The winners are integrated platform vendors. CrowdStrike (Flex ARR +101%) and Palo Alto (NGS ARR $9.10B after acquiring CyberArk and Chronosphere) are absorbing budgets. That said, CrowdStrike's forward P/E of 191x already prices in flawless execution.

  3. Zscaler, Okta and SentinelOne are cheaper on a relative basis, but each faces slowing growth or profitability issues. AhnLab is a domestic beneficiary of N2SF (easing of network separation rules) and EDR demand, and its agentic AI platform launch on Dec 1 is a near-term catalyst.

1. Threat landscape: attacks are shifting from "files" to "privileges and agents"

The core change is that attackers, instead of planting malicious files, are increasingly borrowing legitimate privileges and legitimate tools.

  • AI-automated attacks: AI agents have reached the stage where they carry out reconnaissance, vulnerability exploitation and lateral movement without human involvement (SentinelOne). As attack speed outpaces human response, automation on the defensive side has become essential.

  • Fileless attacks (LotL): Attackers abuse tools built into Windows instead of traditional malware (Huntress). This is an area that signature-based antivirus structurally struggles to catch.

  • Identity theft: With remote work and the cloud erasing the perimeter, identity-centric security that continuously verifies "who you are" has become the standard (TierPoint).

  • AI agents as a new attack surface: An April 2026 survey found that enterprise agent deployments grew about 2x in half a year, but only 19.7% had full security controls in place at deployment (Gravitee, 2026-04). Agent credentials are becoming the most valuable targets in the infrastructure (AppSecure).

  • Supply chain: Malware entering through third-party and SaaS integrations and through AI code suggestions is on the rise. Quantitative data needs verification.

4. Winner structure: platforms win, point products become acquisition targets

Pricing power is shifting to the platform companies that hold the most data. AI security only performs well when endpoint, identity, cloud and network data are brought together in one place.

  • Evidence of platformization: 51% of CrowdStrike customers use 6 or more modules, and 26% use 8 or more. Falcon Flex, its consumption-based contract, has ARR of $2.29B (+101%). Palo Alto said net revenue retention (NRR) for its platformized customers exceeds 120%, and it targets NGS ARR of $20B by FY30.

  • The fate of point-product vendors: Most large deals in 2026 were platforms buying point products. PANW acquired CyberArk (about $25B, closed in February), Google acquired Wiz ($32B, closed in March), and CrowdStrike acquired SGNL ($740M) and Seraphic ($420M). AI agent security startups are likely to follow the same path (estimate).

  • Exception zones: Areas that do not overlap head-on with Big Tech (for example, compliance tailored to finance and the public sector, or on-premises-only environments) leave room for specialist vendors to hold out. Korea's public and financial markets are a prime example.

In conclusion, we rank pricing power as PANW and CRWD > Microsoft (downward pressure through bundle pricing) > ZS and OKTA > S (author's judgment).

Sources: CRWD Q2 FY27, PANW completes CyberArk acquisition, Alphabet completes Wiz acquisition, CNBC SGNL.

One-line view by company (author's judgment)

  • CrowdStrike: Net new ARR hit a record ($332.8M, +51%) and is reaccelerating. With the stock up +166% over 6 months, expectations are already high, so even a small disappointment could bring sharp volatility.

  • Palo Alto: The broadest platform, having added identity (CyberArk→Idira) and observability (Chronosphere) through M&A. FY27 guidance calls for revenue growth of +23–24% and an FCF margin of 38%. The organic growth rate is not disclosed and needs verification.

  • Zscaler: FY27 growth guidance slows to about 17%, and it is cutting about 3% of its workforce. On the other hand, its multiple is among the lowest, so there is room for a rerating if AI security bookings (up more than 50% QoQ) start showing up in results.

  • SentinelOne: Its AI security suite is growing fast, but net new ARR grew only +4% and FCF is negative. Its potential as an acquisition target should be weighed alongside its standalone prospects (estimate).

  • Okta: Growth is the slowest, but cash generation (FCF 28%) is strong. The company itself said agent identity revenue will be "minimal" in FY27, so we see a meaningful contribution from FY28 onward.

  • AhnLab: Double-digit growth in overseas revenue, led by its Saudi joint venture Rakeen, and triple-digit growth in EDR and XTG zero-trust products. It is better approached as a beneficiary of domestic regulation than compared directly with global platforms.

6. What is distinctive about the Korean market

In Korea, the network separation rules in place for 17 years are being replaced by N2SF (National Network Security Framework) from 2026, opening up replacement demand of its own that differs from the global market.

  • From network separation to data classification: N2SF sorts data into 3 tiers, Classified (C), Sensitive (S) and Open (O), and allows Open-tier data to be used in the cloud and in generative AI. The guidelines define 6 domains and 274 detailed security control items (GTT Korea, 2026-03).

  • Government support is still small: KISA-led programs total KRW 5.5 billion (KRW 4.5 billion for adoption support, KRW 990 million for pilots, up to KRW 750 million per project) (Dream Security). We expect the market to take shape in earnest from 2027, when public agencies' own budgets kick in (estimate).

  • Financial sector: As in the case of IBK Industrial Bank of Korea, an approach that combines data classification with zero trust to open Open-tier data to generative AI is spreading. The timeline for revising the Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulations needs verification.

  • On-premises preference: Public and financial institutions still show strong demand for on-premises deployments, which favors domestic vendors over cloud-centric US platforms. This is why AhnLab will release its agentic AI platform on Dec 1 in both on-premises and SaaS versions (SmartBiz).

  • Domestic vendors expanding abroad: AhnLab plans to invest KRW 100 billion over 3 years, targeting revenue of KRW 1 trillion by 2035 with an overseas share of 30% or more. Overseas growth is currently driven by its Saudi joint venture Rakeen, so dependence on the Middle East is high. Annualizing H1 consolidated revenue of KRW 130.4 billion gives roughly KRW 270 billion for the year (estimate); on that basis, the KRW 1 trillion target is an aggressive one that would require about 15% average annual growth over 9 years.

7. Risks

The biggest risk is Microsoft's bundling, followed by high valuations themselves.

  • Big Tech bundling: Microsoft included Security Copilot in M365 E5 at no extra cost, and in May 2026 launched M365 E7 ($99 per user per month), which bundles Copilot, Agent 365 and Entra Suite (Microsoft Security Blog). About 40 million agents are registered in Agent 365. It has 1.6 million security customers (MSFT IR). Microsoft does not separately disclose its latest security revenue, so this needs verification. Google has become a direct competitor in cloud security through its Wiz acquisition.

  • Valuation: CrowdStrike trades at a forward P/E of 191x and Palo Alto at 94.6x. Both stocks rose more than 2x over 6 months (Benzinga, 2026-09-30), so they could fall sharply if results, even when on target, fall short of expectations.

  • Price competition: Platform vendors are squeezing point-product vendors' pricing through bundle discounts and consumption-based contracts (Flex). ZS's slowing growth and S's stalling net new ARR may be signs of this (estimate).

  • AI false positives and autonomous-response incidents: If AI mistakes normal business activity for an attack and blocks it automatically, operations can grind to a halt. As the 2024 CrowdStrike update outage showed, security software itself can cause a major incident. This is why the industry keeps human verification on top of AI decisions (Huntress).

  • Regulation: There are no AI agent security standards yet, so product requirements may change. In Korea, the pace of N2SF adoption and the timing of the Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulations revision are the key variables for domestic security stocks.
